Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Covina

Wood floors react to water quickly. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

What It Looks Like

  • Earthy odor at floor level even though the surface looks clean
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • Sections of flooring that have risen, forming a tent shape along a seam
  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Goes Under

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

The edges come up as each board expands. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

After a week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

Our Process

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Moisture

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Monitor and Advise

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

Water That Keeps Moving

Once below the boards, water moves. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Growth Beneath the Floor

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.
